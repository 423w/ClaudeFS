# L5.2: Gain, saturation, distortion, bitcrush & oversampling (CFS)
*Date started / finished:* 2026-11-19 / 2026-11-19  ·  *Built by:* Claude

## Goal
The FX chain's first slot: a **Drive** (four waveshapers, a pre/post "tone" emphasis, automatic loudness
matching, a dry/wet mix) that runs at **1×, 2× or 4×** through linear-phase halfband filters, and right after
it a **Crush** (bit depth + sample-rate reduction, deliberately aliased). Oversampling is the first thing in CFS
that delays the signal, so the plugin now reports its latency to the host (`clap.latency`) and asks for a
restart when it changes.

## Predictions (written before any L5.2 code)
1. **Aliasing, sine into the shaper.** A 2.6 kHz sine at 48 kHz, driven +24 dB into each shaper. A symmetric
   shaper makes odd harmonics only; everything above 24 kHz folds back onto non-harmonic frequencies.
   - **Hard clip**: a clipped sine has a kink (a jump in the slope), so its harmonics fall as 1/n²
     (power 1/n⁴). The harmonics that fold into the audio band are of order ~10+ at 1×, ~20+ at 2×, ~40+ at 4×;
     doubling the order of that band divides its power by ~8: **about −9 dB per doubling**. Guess:
     **1× −35 dB, 2× −44 dB, 4× −53 dB** (alias power relative to the whole signal).
   - **Soft (tanh)**: analytic (no kink anywhere), so its harmonics eventually fall faster than any power of n:
     each doubling should gain **more** than the last. Guess: **1× −45 dB, 2× −70 dB, 4× < −90 dB**, where the
     halfband's stopband (≈ −95 dB) becomes the floor.
   - **Fold** (a sine folder): smooth too, but its bandwidth grows with the drive (it's FM-like: sin(g·sin)):
     at +24 dB it reaches far past 4×'s Nyquist. Guess: the worst of the four at every factor, **1× −20 dB**,
     and oversampling helps least.
2. **Latency.** A halfband FIR of N = 4m + 3 taps delays by (N − 1)/2 samples at the high rate, and the
   signal goes through it twice (up, down). Stage 1 (N = 63): 31 samples at 2× each way = **31 samples at the
   base rate for 2×**. Stage 2 (N = 23): 11 + 11 samples at 4× = 5.5 base samples, plus one 2×-rate sample of
   padding to make it whole: **37 samples for 4×**. In samples, the same at every sample rate (0.65 / 0.77 ms
   at 48 kHz). An impulse through Hard drive at a level below the clip point should come out as a symmetric
   pulse **peaking at exactly 31 / 37**.
3. **Transparency below the clip point.** Hard clip is exactly linear below 1, and the tone control is a
   pre-emphasis with its *exact* inverse after the shaper, so at 1× a quiet signal through Hard drive should
   come out **equal to the input to float rounding (< −120 dB error)**, at any tone setting. At 2×/4× it should
   equal the input delayed by the latency, minus the filters' passband droop: at 48 kHz < 0.05 dB below
   19 kHz; at 44.1 kHz −1.2 dB at 20 kHz (up + down, both in their transition band).
4. **Loudness.** Without compensation a +24 dB drive is far louder at low input levels and about the same at
   high ones ("louder sounds better"). My auto gain matches the loudness of a −12 dBFS sine exactly; on real
   patches (E-piano, Brass) I expect it within **±3 dB** across drive 0–36 dB.
5. **Mix law: linear.** The driven signal is time-aligned with the dry one (the dry path is delayed by the
   latency too) and mostly in phase with it, so they add as amplitudes: the linear law. If I forgot to delay the
   dry path at 4×, a 50 % mix would be a 37-sample comb filter: a notch at 48000/74 ≈ **650 Hz**.
6. **Bitcrush on a bell.** 8 bits: a quantization noise floor around −50 dB that *follows* the decay (it's
   signal-dependent: silent when the bell is silent, gritty while it rings). Rate 6 kHz: the bell's partials
   above 3 kHz fold down to inharmonic ones, a metallic, ring-modulated sound. "Deliberate aliasing".
7. **CPU** (Release, stereo, per sample at 48 kHz): 1× ≈ 30 ns (two tanh), 2× ≈ 100 ns, 4× ≈ 200 ns.

## What actually happened
1. **Aliasing: Hard right, Soft far better than predicted, Fold wrong.** Alias power of a 2599 Hz sine (amp
   0.25) re the whole signal, auto gain off (`fss_render l5.2`; [ ] = only the part below 16 kHz):

   | shaper, drive | 1× | 2× | 4× |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | Hard +24 dB | −32.3 [−33.5] | −42.7 [−44.5] | −55.6 [−58.4] |
   | Soft +24 dB | −38.7 [−45.4] | −99.0 [−105.9] | −120.1 [−144.8] |
   | Tube +24 dB | −37.4 [−46.8] | −61.8 [−109.0] | −61.8 [−127.7] |
   | Fold +24 dB | −82.6 [−114.2] | −130.3 | −128.6 |
   | any, +12 dB | −100 to −133 | ≤ −119 | ≤ −119 |

   - **Hard**: predicted −35/−44/−53, measured −32/−43/−56: about **−10 to −13 dB per doubling**, the 1/n² kink
     law, with no floor in sight. Hard clipping is the one shaper that still aliases audibly at 4×.
   - **Soft**: predicted 1× −45, 2× −70; measured **−39 and −99**. A smooth shaper's spectrum really does fall
     faster than any power: one doubling buys **60 dB**. At 4× it reaches the measurement's own floor.
   - **Fold**: predicted "the worst". At +24 dB on a 0.25 sine it's the *best* at 1×. A sine folder on a sine
     is sin(β·sin): its spectrum is the Bessel functions Jₙ(β) of L1.4, with β = 3.96 here, and those vanish
     beyond n ≈ β + 1 = 5 harmonics (13 kHz). It's *band-limited*. It only aliases once β·f0 nears Nyquist
     (more drive or higher notes), and then the aliases arrive all at once.
   - **Tube** at 2× and 4×: **−61.8 dB, both**: a floor that isn't the shaper. Below 16 kHz it's −109/−128 dB.
     Even harmonics (Tube makes them; the symmetric shapers don't) land in the stage-1 halfband's transition
     band, 24 to 28.8 kHz, where it attenuates by only 0 to 95 dB, and fold to 19.2 to 24 kHz. That's the bright
     "V" at the top of every 2×/4× spectrogram (`renders/l5.2/sweep_hard_1x_2x_4x.png`). Inaudible, and the
     price of a 63-tap filter; a longer one would shrink it and add latency.
2. **Latency: right.** The impulse peaks at exactly **+31 (2×) and +37 (4×)** samples at 44.1, 48 and 96 kHz,
   matching the reported latency; the tests also check that the impulse response is symmetric about it.
3. **Transparency: right.** Hard below the clip point: error **−143 dB** at 1× (any tone), −117 dB at 4× vs the
   delayed input. Passband (2×, up + down): 48 kHz −0.000 dB at 19 kHz, −0.03 dB at 20 kHz; 44.1 kHz −0.18 dB at
   19 kHz and **−1.19 dB at 20 kHz** (predicted −1.2).
4. **Loudness: my first auto gain was off by 6 to 13 dB.** Level change vs the dry patch, Soft, 2×:

   | | drive 0 | 12 | 24 | 36 dB |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | E-piano, no compensation | −1.6 | +5.3 | +7.9 | +8.7 |
   | E-piano, v1 (fixed law, −12 dBFS sine) | −1.5 | −4.9 | −6.3 | −6.2 |
   | E-piano, v2 (measured, shipped) | +0.6 | +1.2 | +1.4 | +1.5 |
   | Brass, no compensation | −3.4 | +0.0 | +0.9 | +1.2 |
   | Brass, v1 | −3.2 | −10.2 | −13.4 | −13.7 |
   | Brass, v2 | +0.1 | +0.1 | +0.1 | +0.1 |

   The fixed law made a −12 dBFS sine come out at its own loudness. But the signal *inside* the chain is hotter
   than the output suggests (the Output level comes after the FX), and a chord is denser than a sine, so it
   over-compensated. v2 measures instead: two 200 ms energy followers, the (latency-aligned) input and the shaped
   output, gain = √(in/out). Within ±1.5 dB on both patches, and the auto-gain test passes at amplitudes 0.03,
   0.25 and 0.9 for every shaper and drive. (The remaining +1.5 dB on the E-piano: the followers match 200 ms
   windows, and saturation lengthens the decays, which a whole-file RMS counts.)
   Two bugs on the way, both about time: the input follower must read the *delayed* input (at 4× the output
   follower saw 37 samples of silence at every onset and the gain spiked to 3.9×), and the ratio must be applied
   in the sample it's computed in (otherwise the first samples after a reset are uncompensated).
5. **Mix law: right.** A 50 % mix at 4×, at 649 Hz (where an unaligned dry path would notch): **−0.008 dB**.
6. **Bitcrush on the bell**: `l5.2_bell_crush8.wav` (8 bits), `_crush_rate.wav` (6 kHz), `_crush_both.wav`
   (6 bits, 8 kHz). Not measured beyond the unit test (grid and hold exact); how it sounds is the owner's call.
7. **CPU** (Release, ns per stereo sample at 48 kHz): Soft 1× **57**, 2× **156**, 4× **231** (1.1 % of a core);
   Hard 4× 156; Crush 11. Predicted 30 / 100 / 200: the 1× path costs more than two tanh because of the
   shelves, the followers and the dry ring. The Engine with both new slots off: **no change** against b6d1d64,
   both built the same way with cl (FM pad 371 vs 371 µs per 16-voice block, E-piano chorus 195 vs 195).
8. **Tests 209/209** in Debug and Release (11 drive tests, 1 CLAP latency test). Block-size invariance through
   the Engine with drive at 4× and crush on, type/drive/tone/oversampling changing mid-stream: bit-identical.
   Real-time audit 0 allocations (its random parameter changes now also flip the oversampling). clap-validator
   34/34; `l1.7_piece.wav` `93d602f0…` unchanged; the factory bank regenerated (new ids at their defaults: no
   sound change).

**Exit criteria.**
- Measured aliasing at 1×, 2× and 4× ✅ (table above, plus sweeps and a spectrogram).
- Latency reported correctly when oversampling is on ✅: `clap.latency` returns 31/37; a change requests a
  restart and is applied (and announced with `latency.changed`) in `activate` (tested with a fake host).
  **Not verified in FL Studio**: whether FL compensates CLAP latency and honours `request_restart` (FL steps).

## How it works
**Oversampling** (`dsp/halfband.h`): linear-phase halfband FIRs, Kaiser-windowed sinc, polyphase (half the taps
are zero, and the upsampler's odd outputs are a pure delay). Stage 1 is 63 taps (base ↔ 2×), stage 2 is 23 taps
(2× ↔ 4×). Latency 31 / 37 samples, whole by construction (one 2×-rate sample of padding at 4×).

**Drive** (`fx/drive.h`): pre-emphasis shelf → drive gain → shaper inside the oversampler → DC blocker (Tube
only) → the shelf's exact inverse → measured auto gain → output trim → linear mix with a latency-aligned dry
path. The shapers live in `dsp/shapers.h` (Soft tanh, Hard clip, Fold sin, Tube biased tanh), for L4.5 to reuse.

**Crush** (`fx/crush.h`): sample & hold on a double phase accumulator, then a mid-tread quantizer with
fractional bits. Its own slot, right after the drive.

**Latency through the stack.** The effect has a *requested* and a *committed* oversampling factor. The FxSlot
(`fx/effect.h`, new `LatentEffect` concept) delays its own dry path by the committed latency, so on/off is a
crossfade between aligned signals and "off" is a pure delay: switching or automating the drive never changes the
latency. A slot that wakes primes its effect with the 128 input samples it missed (no hole in the fade-in). The
Engine commits a new factor at `prepare()`/`setPatch()`; with `setDeferLatencyChanges(false)` (standalone,
renders, tests) also at the parameter change. The CLAP plugin defers: `process()` sees `latencyPending()` and
calls `host->request_restart()` once; the host deactivates and re-activates; `activate()` commits, stores the new
latency and calls `host_latency->changed()`. The trace's summary line counts the restart requests.

Params: drive 250–257 (on, type, drive dB, tone dB, oversample, auto gain, output dB, mix), crush 260–263 (on,
bits, rate Hz, mix). Defaults: off and **1×** (no latency), so every old patch and project is bit-identical.

## Alternatives rejected
- **IIR (allpass polyphase) halfbands**: ~2 samples of latency instead of 31–37, and cheaper, but non-linear
  phase: the waveform's shape changes, a dry/wet mix combs a little at high frequencies, and there's no whole
  latency to report. For a synth insert at ≤ 0.8 ms, linear phase and an honest latency won.
- **Latency only while the drive is on** (0 when off): then switching the drive, or automating the switch, would
  restart the plugin each time. The latency follows the oversampling setting alone.
- **Default 2×**: cleaner out of the box, but every existing project would suddenly get 31 samples of latency
  and every golden render would shift. 1× by default; presets that drive hard set 2× or 4× themselves.
- **Waking a latent effect without priming it**: its output would start one latency late, a hole in the fade.
- **A static auto-gain law** (v1 above), or none (the "louder is better" trap), or a manual trim only.
- **Crush inside the Drive** (one slot): fewer panels, but two unrelated ideas in one class, and you couldn't
  have the crusher without paying for the drive.

## What surprised me
1. **The band-limited folder.** "Wavefolders alias horribly" is true of folders driven hard; a sine folder fed a
   sine is FM in disguise, and L1.4's Bessel functions say exactly where its spectrum ends.
2. **One doubling buys 60 dB for tanh** and 10 dB for a hard clip. Oversampling is a cure for smooth shapers and
   a palliative for kinked ones (that's what ADAA in L4.5 is for: [PARKER-ADAA]).
3. **The auto gain took three tries**, and both bugs were about time. Testing at three input levels caught
   what one level couldn't.
4. **Tube's −62 dB floor** that no oversampling factor moved: a property of the filter, not of the shaper.

## Decisions made
- No numbered decision. An "L5.2 drive & latency conventions" entry in DECISIONS.md: latency follows the
  oversampling setting only (dry path delayed to match, default 1×), committed only in activate (restart
  request), linear-phase FIR halfbands, measured auto gain, crush as its own slot right after the drive.
