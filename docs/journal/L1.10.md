# L1.10: Filters II: virtual analog (TPT/ZDF SVF) (CFS)
*Date started / finished:* 2026-11-17 / 2026-11-17  ·  *Built by:* Claude

## Goal
The filter CFS actually ships: a TPT state-variable filter that can be modulated hard without zipper or
blow-ups, placed in every voice after the FM operators, with its own envelope, key tracking and velocity.

## Predictions (before building)
1. **Static response:** the SVF's LP/BP/HP/notch equal the RBJ biquad's to ~1e-6 (same analog prototype,
   same bilinear transform, same prewarp). Only the float rounding of `g` should differ.
2. **Torture test** (cutoff swept by a 50 Hz sine from 100 Hz to 15 kHz, Q 10, per-sample updates):
   the SVF output stays bounded with a level close to what a static filter would give; the biquad (both
   forms) produces visibly larger peaks, with TDF2 the worse of the two, maybe bursts 10–20 dB above the
   SVF, but probably not true divergence at Q 10. With random per-sample cutoff/Q (fuzz) the biquad may
   actually blow up; the SVF will not.
3. **Cost:** one `tan` + one divide per sample per voice for coefficient updates, about the same as the
   rest of the filter. At 16 voices that's ≈ 1 % of a core; a candidate hot spot for L2.6, not a problem.
4. **In the voice:** a 4-octave filter envelope on a pluck gives a spectral centroid that starts several kHz
   up and falls with the envelope's decay time; key tracking 1.0 keeps the centroid-to-pitch ratio constant
   across octaves.
5. **Byte-identical regression:** with the filter switched Off (the default for existing patches), every
   earlier render (`l1.7_piece.wav`) stays byte-identical.

## What actually happened
`fss_render l1.10`; brightness with `tools/analyze/brightness.py`.

1. **Static response ✅.** `tests/test_svf.cpp`: LP/BP/HP/notch × 3 cutoffs × 2 Qs, the SVF's computed response
   equals the RBJ biquad's to < 1e-5 (linear) at 40 frequencies, and the measured impulse response matches
   within 0.1 dB.
2. **Torture test** (white noise at −24.8 dB RMS, Q 10, cutoff swept 100 Hz ↔ 15 kHz by a 50 Hz sine):

   | | peak | RMS | non-finite |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | SVF | 0.504 | −21.3 dB | 0 |
   | biquad DF1 | 0.753 | −16.4 dB | 0 |
   | biquad TDF2 | 0.777 | −16.4 dB | 0 |

   Both biquads come out **~5 dB louder** than the SVF on the same input with the same per-sample settings:
   energy no frozen filter would produce, injected by changing coefficients under states that don't match
   them. No blow-up at Q 10 (as predicted); the excess was smaller than my 10–20 dB guess, and DF1 vs TDF2
   barely differ.
3. **Fuzz** (random cutoff 10 Hz–30 kHz and Q 0.5–50 on every sample, 10 s): **SVF peak 0.73, finite.
   Both biquads diverge to ±3e38 and then NaN (≈ 480 000 non-finite samples).** The test suite fuzzes the SVF
   with four seeds and asserts it stays finite and below 1000.
4. **The pluck** (`Reso pluck` preset: C>D feedback-saw, LP Q 5, cutoff 250 Hz, env +5 oct / 300 ms decay,
   key track 0.5, velocity 2 oct). Spectral centroid 20 ms after each onset:

   | note (f0) | C2 65 Hz | C3 131 | C4 262 | C5 523 | C6 1047 |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | key track 0.5 | 592 Hz | 987 | 1532 | 2323 | 3557 |
   | key track 0 | 963 | 1268 | 1532 | 1773 | 2024 |

   With tracking 0 the centroid is 15× f0 on C2 but only 1.9× f0 on C6 (high notes nearly a sine, low notes
   buzzy); tracking 0.5 halves that spread (9× → 3.4×). Prediction 4 was about tracking 1.0; I chose 0.5 for
   the preset because full tracking makes the top end thin by design. The centroid then falls with the
   filter envelope: C2 592 Hz at 20 ms → 106 Hz at 150 ms. Velocity 20 → 127 on C3: centroid at 50 ms
   174 → 449 Hz.
5. **Cutoff knob staircase** (60 Hz, 128-step CC-like values, Q 8, held note): energy above 8 kHz is
   −60.7 dB **with and without smoothing**. A surprise, see below.
6. **Regression ✅:** `l1.7_piece.wav` still hashes `92baa60b…` (filter Off by default).
7. **Tests:** 113/113 in Debug and Release: 3 new SVF tests and a voice-filter test; the block-size
   invariance test's patch now has a resonant filter with envelope, key and velocity tracking (still
   bit-exact); the real-time audit fuzzes the new parameters too (0 allocations).

**Exit criteria.** The SVF survives the torture test with no blow-ups and no zipper ✅ (and the fuzz, which
the biquad doesn't). "A resonant FM-through-filter patch you love": `Reso pluck` exists and measures as
intended; **loving it is for the owner's ears** (Claude can't hear). The paragraph ✅ (below).

## How it works
- `core/include/fss/dsp/svf.h`: Simper's form of the TPT SVF. `set(cutoffHz, q)` computes `g = tan(π·fc/fs)`,
  `k = 1/Q` and the solved loop gains `a1..a3`; `tick(x)` returns LP, BP and HP at once from two states.
  `responseAt()` evaluates the analog prototype at the bilinear-mapped frequency.
- Voice chain (`voice.h`): `FmVoice → Svf → steal fade`. The cutoff is computed per sample in **octaves**:
  `log2(base) + envOctaves·env + keyTrack·(key−60)/12 + velOctaves·(vel−1)`, then `exp2` and `tan`.
  The base cutoff and Q are smoothed once per sample in the Engine (cutoff in octaves, 10 ms) and handed to
  every voice in a `VoiceControl` struct, together with the live algorithm matrix.
- `FilterParams` in `patch.h`; 10 new parameters (ids 10–19, group "Filter"). **CC 74 → cutoff, CC 71 →
  resonance** (General MIDI 2's meaning; they drove op A / op C levels before). `ParamInfo` gained a `group`
  that fss_play turns into section headers, and presets are now a list (`kFactoryPresets`) the GUI iterates.

### Why the TPT SVF beats the biquad for a synth (the exit-criterion paragraph)
A biquad is designed in the frequency domain: its five coefficients give the right response *as long as
they don't change*, and its states are bookkeeping (past samples or partial sums) with no physical meaning.
Change the coefficients every sample and you have a different, time-varying system that can gain energy
from the modulation itself: measured here as +5 dB of spurious output under a 50 Hz sweep, and outright
divergence to infinity under random per-sample settings. The TPT SVF is instead a discretized *circuit*:
two trapezoidal integrators whose states are the energy stored in them, connected by a feedback loop that is
solved exactly each sample rather than delayed. Moving the cutoff changes how fast that energy flows, not the
energy itself, so modulation can't pump energy in, at any rate. On top of that it prewarps (cutoff exact up
to Nyquist), gives LP/BP/HP/notch from one structure, and costs about the same as a biquad.

## Alternatives rejected
- **Biquad with per-sample coefficients** in the voice: measured worse (above), and the RBJ formulas cost a
  `cos`, a `sin` and divisions per update, vs one `tan` for the SVF.
- **Moog ladder** (the optional step 5): not built. One filter type is enough for M4; the ladder (and its
  `tanh` version) fits naturally with the nonlinearity lesson (L4.5).
- **Resonance as 0..100 %.** Q with an exponential knob is honest about units, and equal knob travel gives
  roughly equal dB of peak (20·log10 Q).
- **Smoothing the cutoff in Hz.** A 100 Hz → 10 kHz glide in Hz spends most of its time in the top octave;
  in octaves it moves evenly as heard.
- **A separate amp (VCA) envelope.** The carriers' envelopes already are the amplitude envelope; the filter
  envelope only moves the cutoff.

## What surprised me
1. **The fuzz didn't just distort the biquads, it destroyed them** (infinity, then NaN forever: a NaN state
   never recovers). Real automation never does this, but an audio-rate LFO on a high-Q biquad gets closer than
   you'd think. The SVF's worst case was a peak of 0.73.
2. **Unsmoothed cutoff steps don't click in the SVF.** I expected L2.4's zipper story again; the energy above
   8 kHz is identical with and without smoothing. The states carry through a coefficient change, so a cutoff
   step only changes the *rate* at which the filter evolves: no discontinuity. What smoothing still fixes is
   musical: unsmoothed, a resonant peak walks in 60 Hz steps (a stepped "wah": sidebands around the peak,
   not HF clicks). I kept the 10 ms smoothing for that.
3. Even with key tracking 0 the centroid rises with pitch (963 → 2024 Hz): harmonics are further apart on
   higher notes, so more of the spectrum's weight sits higher under the same cutoff.

## [THINK] answers
- **Where does an analog synth's Q knob map?** To `k = 1/Q`, the damping: how much of the band-pass output is
  fed back against the input. Q 0.5 is two real poles (no peak), 0.707 is maximally flat, ~2–8 is the musical
  "resonant" range, and toward Q 25 (+28 dB) it whistles. k = 0 (infinite Q) would be a lossless resonator:
  in this linear filter it rings forever without growing; real self-oscillation needs a nonlinearity to set
  its amplitude (L4.5).
- **Where should the filter go for FM?** After the carriers are mixed (here, per voice): it shapes the
  *result* of the FM, like a tone control on the note. A filter on a *modulator* changes the modulating
  waveform instead, so it changes *which sidebands exist* (low-passed modulator ≈ purer sine ≈ cleaner
  spectra; high-passed ≈ more modulator harmonics ≈ denser sidebands). A different instrument (L4.2 territory).
