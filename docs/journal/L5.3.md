# L5.3: Delay lines & a tempo-synced delay (CFS)
*Date started / finished:* 2026-11-18 / 2026-11-18  ·  *Built by:* Claude

## Goal
The delay line as a reusable primitive (`dsp::DelayLine`, fractional reads with a choice of interpolation),
and a stereo delay effect on it: time in ms or synced to the host's tempo, feedback with a damping filter,
ping-pong, mix, and a documented, click-free behaviour when the time changes while it plays.

## Predictions (written before any delay code)
1. **Impulse test:** with linear interpolation, an impulse delayed by 10.25 samples comes out as **0.75 at
   sample 10 and 0.25 at 11**; with the 4-point (cubic Lagrange) read, it spreads over **samples 9–12** with a
   small negative lobe, and its peak lands between 10 and 11. Integer delays are exact with every policy.
2. **Linear interpolation darkens fractional delays:** at a fraction of 0.5 it averages two neighbours, a zero
   at Nyquist: **−3 dB at about fs/4** per pass. Through 10 repeats of feedback that's a clearly duller tail
   than an integer delay. So the "clean" delay should read at whole samples (it can: time doesn't change
   between crossfades), and only the gliding "tape" mode needs a fractional read.
3. **Changing the time:**
   - *Clean* (two read taps, 50 ms crossfade): no pitch change, no click; the click meter at the change shows
     nothing above the material's own level. Cost: during the crossfade both the old and new echo sound.
   - *Tape* (the time glides, one-pole ~150 ms): a pitch swoop. Halving the time over ~0.3 s reads the
     buffer up to ~2× faster than real time at the start of the glide: up to roughly **an octave up** for a moment
     on the echoes, never a click.
   - *Neither* (jump the read position): a click per change. I'll render all three.
4. **Tempo sync:** at 120 BPM a dotted eighth is **375 ms = 18000 samples** at 48 kHz, exactly. In the plugin
   the time follows the host's tempo; with no transport (fss_play, fss_render) it uses 120 BPM.
5. **Tail:** at feedback 0.35, echoes fall by 9.1 dB per repeat, so 90 dB takes **10 repeats**: 3.75 s at
   375 ms. At feedback 0.9, ~99 repeats: 37 s. The tail CLAP reports must follow the knob.
6. **Ping-pong:** the first echo is on the **left only**, the second on the right, alternating. A mono synth
   becomes stereo, so L5.1's width knob finally does something to the echoes.

## What actually happened
1. **Impulse test: right.** Linear gives exactly 0.75 at sample 10 and 0.25 at 11. Cubic (Lagrange) gives
   −0.055, 0.82, 0.27, −0.039 at samples 9–12, summing to 1. Integer delays are exact with all three policies
   (`test_delay.cpp`).
2. **Linear darkening: right, to the decimal.** An impulse looped through a gain-1 line, the 10th echo against
   the 1st at fs/4 (`fss_render l5.3`): integer delay 0.0 dB; fraction 0.25 **−2.04 dB per pass**; fraction 0.5
   **−3.01 dB per pass** (−27 dB after 9 passes). That's why Clean reads at whole samples.
3. **Changing the time** (`click_meter.py --channel 0 --at 2.0`, energy above 2 kHz at the change):
   Clean **−106 dB** (nothing: the material's own level); Tape **−63.5 dB**; naive jump **−59 dB**.
   The meter can't tell Tape's deliberate octave swoop (a pitch-up moves energy above 2 kHz) from a click.
   The unit test can: a 200 Hz sine with two time changes mid-echo never steps by more than 0.12 per sample in
   either mode (a jump steps by up to ~2). *How Tape sounds is for the owner's ears.*
4. **Tempo sync: right.** At 120 BPM, 1/8 D is exactly 18000 samples; at 100 BPM, 21600; at 90 BPM via the
   CLAP transport, 24000 (the plugin test reads it back through `clap.tail`). A mid-block CLAP_EVENT_TRANSPORT
   is applied at its frame. `l5.3_tempo_change.wav`: 120 → 90 BPM at 2 s, the echoes move from 375 to 500 ms.
5. **Tail: right.** Feedback 35 %: 11 × 18001 samples (10 repeats to −90 dB, plus the first echo). 90 %:
   100 × 18001 ≈ 37.5 s. Reported through `clap.tail`.
6. **Ping-pong: right.** The first echo is left only (exactly 1.0 on L, 0.0 on R), then R × feedback, then
   L × fb². The e-piano render is now stereo (max |L − R| = 0.09).
7. **Spill** (`l5.3_spill.wav`, delay switched off at 1.0 s): the echoes keep decaying (−39, −45, −55 dB over
   the following second), and a note played at 2.5 s has L = R: no echo, because the delay isn't fed any more.
8. **A cost I didn't predict:** when the FX slot puts the delay to sleep it calls `reset()`, which clears 2 × 4 s
   of buffer: **29 µs at 48 kHz, 210 µs at 192 kHz**, once, on the audio thread. It's bounded and rare (only after
   a full tail of silence), but at 192 kHz with 256-sample blocks it's 16 % of one block. See open questions.
9. **Tests 179/179** in Debug and Release (8 new). Block-size invariance through the Engine with the delay on,
   two tempo changes, a time change, Clean → Tape and an off/on spill: blocks of 1, 64, 333 and 4096
   bit-identical. Real-time audit 0 allocations; clap-validator 34/34; `l1.7_piece.wav` `93d602f0…` unchanged.

**Exit criterion:** a tempo-synced ping-pong delay with no clicks when changing time while playing, with the
chosen behaviour documented ✅ (Clean by default, Tape as an option, both measured above).

## How it works
- **`dsp/delay_line.h`**: `DelayLine<Interp>`, a power-of-two `std::vector` (allocated in `prepare`) with a
  mask instead of a modulo, `push()` and `read<I>(delay)`. Interpolation is a policy type (`NoInterp`,
  `LinearInterp`, `CubicInterp`). `read<I>` lets one line be read with a cheaper policy than the one it was
  sized for: the delay's Clean taps read whole samples from a line sized for linear.
  Read-before-write convention: in a feedback loop you read `d − 1`, then push input + feedback.
- **`fx/delay.h`**: `StereoDelay`. Per sample: read (Clean: one or two whole-sample taps with a 50 ms linear
  crossfade; Tape: one linear tap gliding at 150 ms), damp (a one-pole in the loop), write (the straight and
  ping-pong routings blended by a smoothed amount), mix (equal power). Time = `beats × 60 / BPM` or ms, clamped
  to 1 ms..4 s, and in Clean rounded to whole samples. Tail = time × repeats to −90 dB.
- **Tempo** is a new `Event::Type::Tempo`, not a parameter: the song owns it, not the patch. The Engine keeps
  `tempoBpm_` across `reset()`/`setPatch()`; the default is 120 BPM (`Engine::kDefaultTempoBpm`).
  - CLAP: `clap_process.transport` at the start of each block and `CLAP_EVENT_TRANSPORT` inside it become a
    Tempo event *only when the tempo changed* (hosts send it every block). The trace summary now reports
    `tempo first -> last BPM (n changes)`.
  - `fss_play`: a **BPM** field next to Panic (default 120). `fss_render`: 120, or Tempo events in a scenario.
- **Params 210–218**: on, sync, note (14 divisions, a menu), time (ms), feedback (%, max 95), damping (Hz),
  ping-pong, glide (Clean/Tape), mix (%). The editor's FX page got the Delay panel for free (it's generated
  from the table). Captions now drop their panel's name ("Delay feedback" → "Feedback").

## Alternatives rejected
- **Tape only** (smooth the time, accept the pitch bend): simplest, and many delays do it, but a synced delay
  would then swoop on every tempo change. **Clean only**: loses the musical knob-turn warble. Each is ~20
  lines, so both are offered, Clean by default.
- **Fractional reads everywhere** (one code path): measured −3 dB per pass at fs/4 with linear interpolation
  at a half-sample fraction. A synced time is almost never a whole number of samples, so every repeat would
  darken by a different, tempo-dependent amount. Rounding costs at most 10 µs of timing error.
- **Tempo as a parameter** (automatable, in the patch): wrong owner. A preset shouldn't carry a tempo, and the
  host would show a "tempo" knob nobody should turn.
- **Restarting the crossfade on every new time**: then a knob sweep never finishes a crossfade and the old tap
  never fades out. Waiting for the running crossfade to end turns a sweep into clean 50 ms steps.
- **Allpass interpolation**: flat magnitude, but it has state, so a moving delay smears. Not needed yet.

## What surprised me
1. **Prediction 2 came out exactly** (−3.01 dB per pass). The textbook says it; measuring it made "integer
   reads for Clean" an obvious decision instead of a fussy one.
2. **Hosts send the tempo with every block.** Without the "only on change" filter, every block would retarget
   the delay (harmless, but noise in the Engine's event list).
3. **The sleep reset cost**: invisible at 48 kHz, worth a thought at 192 kHz.

- **Time changes:** what does your delay do when you turn the time knob, slowly and quickly? Click, swoop, or step?
- **Interpolation:** linear, cubic, or none? Loop an impulse through yours with feedback 1 at a half-sample
  delay and measure the 10th echo; compare with my −27 dB.
- **Tempo:** where does your tempo live (event, parameter, global)? Does it follow a tempo change *inside* a block?
- **Ping-pong:** is your first echo on one side only? Does switching ping-pong while echoes ring click?
- **Bypass:** does off stop the echoes, or let them ring out?

## Experiments to try
1. In `fx/delay.h`, read the Clean taps with `dsp::LinearInterp` instead of `NoInterp`, render
   `l5.3_epiano_delay.wav` at feedback 80 %, and compare the tail's brightness with the original.
2. Saturate the feedback write (`fb * lpL_` → `std::tanh(1.5f * fb * lpL_) / 1.5f`): a dub delay that
   saturates at high feedback instead of growing.
3. Set `kCrossfadeMs` to 5 and to 200: at 5 ms the change gets a faint tick; at 200 ms both echoes sound at once.

## Open questions / for later
- The 192 kHz reset cost (210 µs): if it matters, clear lazily (track the furthest position written since the
  last reset) or clear in slices over the following blocks.
- Saturation and a high-pass in the loop (dub-style thinning) are natural additions.
- FL Studio tempo automation: CFS follows each block's transport tempo; the owner should check a tempo ramp.
