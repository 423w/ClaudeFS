# L5.4: Modulated delays: chorus, flanger, phaser (CFS)
*Date started / finished:* 2026-11-18 / 2026-11-18  ·  *Built by:* Claude

## Goal
Three modulation effects in the FX chain, each its own slot: a multi-voice stereo **chorus** (and the FM
e-piano + chorus pairing as a factory preset), a feedback **flanger**, and an allpass **phaser**. Then explain,
with a measurement, why a phaser isn't a flanger.

## Predictions (written before any L5.4 code)
1. **Chorus pitch wobble.** A delay that moves by `d(t) = D + (W/2)·sin(2π·r·t)` shifts the pitch by the factor
   `1 − d'(t)`, peaking at `π·r·W`. With rate 0.6 Hz and width 3 ms that's 0.0057: **±9.8 cents**. That's what a
   chorus *is*: a few slightly detuned copies. At 5 Hz the same width is ±80 cents: vibrato, not chorus.
2. **Interpolation under modulation.** Linear interpolation's gain depends on the fractional position α:
   |H| = |(1 − α) + α·e^(−jω)|, which is lowest at α = ½: cos(ω/2) = **−0.47 dB at 5 kHz**. A moving delay sweeps
   α through 0..1 over and over, so a 5 kHz sine through one chorus voice gets a level ripple of about
   **0.5 dB** (an AM at the sweep rate: error products roughly **35–45 dB** below the tone). Cubic Lagrange:
   ripple under **0.05 dB**, products below **−60 dB**.
3. **Flanger combs are harmonic.** A delay τ mixed 50/50 with dry puts notches at **(k + ½)/τ**: at τ = 1 ms
   they're at 500, 1500, 2500 Hz..., evenly spaced on a linear axis (so they look bunched up at the top on a log
   plot). Positive feedback sharpens the peaks between them; negative feedback moves the peaks to where the
   notches were.
4. **Phaser notches aren't.** N first-order allpasses at break frequency fc give a phase of −N·2·atan(f/fc):
   a notch wherever that reaches an odd multiple of −π. For **6 stages, 3 notches**, at
   fc·tan(π/12), fc·tan(3π/12), fc·tan(5π/12) = **0.27·fc, 1·fc, 3.7·fc**: spaced by ratio, not by Hz, and
   only N/2 of them, not an endless comb. That's the "why a phaser isn't a flanger" answer, and the spectrum
   plot should show it.
5. **Mix laws:** these three are correlated with the dry signal (short delays, allpasses), so they use the
   **linear** mix: at 50 %, notches are as deep as they can be (dry and wet cancel exactly where they're in
   anti-phase).
6. **The e-piano + chorus** becomes stereo (L ≠ R from the different LFO phases) and its mono sum keeps its
   level within ~1 dB (no big comb-filter dip on mono speakers, because the voices move).

## What actually happened
1. **Chorus pitch wobble:** not measured directly (it's the formula), but the tests pin the geometry: with
   depth 0 every voice is an exact 10 ms delay (480 samples), and a voice-count change 2 → 4 → 1 on a 300 Hz
   sine never steps by more than 0.05 per sample (a voice appearing at once would step by ~0.5).
2. **Interpolation under modulation: ripple right, error wrong.** A 5 kHz sine through a hand-made chorus
   voice (10 + 1.5·(1 + sin) ms at 0.6 Hz), against the exact analytic answer, measured at the sine's crests:
   - linear: level ripple **0.47 dB** (prediction: 0.47, cos(ω/2) at a half-sample fraction), error **−28.3 dB**
   - cubic: level ripple **0.04 dB** (prediction: < 0.05), error **−50.6 dB**
   The ripple is exactly the theory; the error is 7–10 dB worse than I guessed for both, because the error
   includes the timing (phase) error, not only the level. Cubic costs 18 % more CPU (178 vs 151 ns per sample
   for 4 voices) for 22 dB less error: it stays the default.
3. **Flanger: half right.** The frozen 1 ms comb notches 500 Hz and 1.5 kHz (< 0.01) and passes 1 kHz and
   2 kHz at exactly 1.0; feedback 0.5 lifts the 1 kHz peak to 1.5 and deepens the 500 Hz notch to 0.167, as the
   algebra says. **Wrong:** "negative feedback swaps peaks and notches". In a 50 % mix it gives 0.83 at 1 kHz
   and 0.5 at 500 Hz: the feedback path's resonances move to 500 Hz, which *fills* the notches instead of
   swapping them. The swap is true of the wet path alone. (I caught it doing the algebra for the test,
   before running it; the test now checks the right numbers.)
4. **Phaser: right.** 6 stages at 1 kHz: notches at **268, 1000 and 3666 Hz**, and a sweep from 50 Hz to 20 kHz
   finds **exactly 3**. (3666, not 3732: the TPT allpass is a bilinear transform, which squeezes the top of the
   spectrum. 268 and 1000 hardly move, because 1 kHz is where the warping is pre-compensated.)
   `flanger_vs_phaser.png` shows both: 24 evenly spaced comb teeth below 24 kHz vs 3 notches evenly spaced by ratio.
5. **Mix laws: wrong for the chorus, and it was my L5.1 call.** With the linear law, "E-piano chorus" at 50 %
   came out **5.5 dB quieter** in mono than "E-piano". A copy 10 ms late is many periods away at every pitch an
   e-piano plays, and it keeps moving, so dry and wet are *uncorrelated*: they add as powers (−3 dB at 50/50 with
   linear gains), and averaging two uncorrelated voices by amplitude costs another 3 dB. Fixed: the chorus now
   uses the **equal-power** mix and normalizes its voices by **power** (1/√N). The mono sum is now **−2.0 dB**
   against the dry patch; the rest is the stereo itself (L and R wet are uncorrelated too, so half their power
   lives in the side signal: side/mid −1.0 dB). The flanger and phaser keep the linear law: their sound *is*
   the interference. `effect.h`'s mix-law comment now says so, and says how we found out.
6. **The e-piano is stereo now**, and wider with width at 130 % (the "E-piano chorus" preset). Whether it
   "sounds classic" is the owner's to judge (FL steps below).
7. **CPU** (Release, ns per stereo sample, % of one core at 48 kHz): chorus 2 voices 94 ns (0.45 %), 4 voices
   178 ns (0.86 %); flanger 45 ns (0.22 %); phaser 207 ns (0.99 %: 2 `tan` + 2 `exp2` per sample, 24 allpasses).
8. **Tests 185/185** in Debug and Release (6 new). Block-size invariance through the Engine with all three on
   and their voice/stage counts changing: bit-identical. Real-time audit 0 allocations; clap-validator 34/34;
   `l1.7_piece.wav` `93d602f0…` unchanged (E-piano itself is untouched; the chorus version is a new preset).

## How it works
**Chorus** (`fx/chorus.h`): N voices (1–4), each a cubic read from a delay swept by a sine LFO between `delay`
and `delay + depth`, with fixed phase offsets (0, ½, ¼, ¾ cycle) and the right channel 90° further on. A
moving read is a pitch shift: speed 1 − d′(t), peak π·rate·depth (±10 cents at 0.6 Hz and 3 ms). The voice
count fades voices in and out (fixed offsets, so no existing voice moves). Equal-power mix.

**Flanger** (`fx/flanger.h`): one cubic read from a 0.1–20 ms delay swept by an LFO, with feedback (−95..95 %),
linear mix. Delay + dry = comb: notches at (k + ½)/τ, all the way up the spectrum, evenly spaced in Hz: a
harmonic series, which is why a resonant flanger seems to sing a pitch.

**Phaser** (`fx/phaser.h`): 12 TPT first-order allpasses per channel (always all running), the output tapped
after 2–12 of them (a stage-count change crossfades between two taps over 20 ms), feedback from the tap to the
input, linear mix. The break frequency sweeps exponentially around `center`. N stages: N/2 notches, at
fc·tan((2k+1)π/2N), spaced by ratio.

**Why a phaser isn't a flanger**, in one sentence: a flanger mixes in a *delayed* copy (phase −2πfτ, a straight
line in f, so the notches repeat forever, evenly in Hz), while a phaser mixes in an *allpassed* copy (phase
−2N·atan(f/fc), which only reaches −Nπ, so there are N/2 notches, evenly spaced by ratio, and no pitch).

**Chain order** (D16, still fixed): chorus → flanger → phaser → delay → width → output. Params: chorus
220–225, flanger 230–235, phaser 240–246. The FX page shows five panels; nothing in the editor had to change.

**New tool**: `tools/analyze/plot_fx_response.py`: the magnitude response of effects from impulse-response WAVs,
on a linear and a log axis, plus their notch frequencies.

## Alternatives rejected
- **One "Mod FX" slot with a mode switch** (chorus / flanger / phaser, as many synths do): fewer parameters,
  but a mode switch is an algorithm swap (it needs its own crossfade) and you can't stack a phaser into a
  chorus. Three effects, three files, also compare better with the owner's version.
- **k/N phase offsets** for the chorus voices (evenly spaced for any count): changing the count would move every
  voice's phase, a delay jump, a click. Fixed offsets are evenly spaced for 2 and 4 voices, and nearly so for 3.
- **A triangle LFO** (the Juno's): its constant slope gives a constant pitch offset that flips sign, a
  "two detuned copies" sound; the sine is smoother. One line to try (experiments).
- **Recomputing the phaser only for the active stages**: then a stage-count change would wake stages with stale
  state. Running all 12 costs ~100 ns per sample and makes the crossfade trivial.
- **Linear interpolation for the chorus** (18 % cheaper): 22 dB more error and an audible high-frequency
  shimmer on bright sounds (FM is bright).

## What surprised me
1. **I got the chorus mix law wrong twice in one lesson**: in L5.1's comment ("a short chorus is correlated") and
   in prediction 6. Correlation is about *period vs delay*, not "short vs long": 10 ms is long compared with a
   300 Hz period. A flanger's 1 ms is short compared with... the low partials, but its *sound* is the comb, so
   linear is right there for a different reason. The measurement settled it in one render.
2. **The heap corruption was in the test, not the effect.** A test loop processing 256-sample blocks over 48000
   samples writes 128 samples past the end. The symptom (a 0.31 "click" on R only) looked exactly like a DSP bug.
3. **The bilinear warping is visible**: the phaser's top notch at 3666 Hz instead of 3732, exactly where the
   prewarped bilinear allpass formula puts it.

## Experiments to try
1. Triangle LFO for the chorus: in `chorus.h`, replace `lfoSine(pk)` with a triangle (`2·|2·cycles − 1| − 1`):
   hear the Juno-style "two detuned copies" against the sine's smoother swirl.
2. Through-zero flanging: also delay the dry path by `delay + depth/2` and let τ swing through it. The notches
   sweep through 0 Hz: the classic tape-flange "zero-crossing" whoosh.
3. Phaser with spread break frequencies (stage k at fc·2^(k/4)): fewer, broader notches, a different colour.
