## Vocabulary, in my own words

A synth is two networks running side by side:

- the **signal path** carries audio (the thing you hear), sample by sample;
- the **control path** carries slow-ish numbers that change the settings of the signal path over time.

Every module we'll build belongs to one of those two, and a few (an LFO at audio rate, an FM modulator)
deliberately blur the line.

| Word | What it is | Signal or control? | Where it lands in CFS |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Oscillator** | Something that repeats a waveform at a given frequency. The *shape* of one cycle sets the timbre; the *rate* sets the pitch. Sine = one harmonic; saw = all harmonics at 1/n; square = odd harmonics at 1/n; triangle = odd at 1/n²; noise = no pitch, energy everywhere. "Brightness" = how much energy sits in the upper harmonics. | signal | `fss::dsp` Phasor/Sine (L1.2), PolyBLEP waves (L1.8) |
| **Operator** | FM word for "a sine oscillator with its own envelope and output level". Its output either goes to the speakers (**carrier**) or into another operator's *phase* (**modulator**). | signal | `fss::synth` Operator (L1.4–L1.5) |
| **Filter** | A frequency-dependent volume knob. Low-pass keeps lows, removes highs above the **cutoff**. **Resonance** boosts a narrow band right at the cutoff, which you hear as a whistle or "wow". High-pass and band-pass are the obvious siblings. | signal (its cutoff is a control input) | SVF / ladder (L1.9–L1.10) |
| **Amplifier (VCA)** | A volume control that the control path can move. With an envelope on it, it decides when a note is audible at all. | signal | Amp stage in the Voice |
| **Waveshaper** | A function applied to each sample (`y = tanh(drive·x)`). Bends the waveform, which adds harmonics. Drive/saturation/distortion are all this. | signal | L4.5, L5.2 |
| **Envelope (ADSR)** | A one-shot control shape triggered by a note: **A**ttack time up to full, **D**ecay time down to the **S**ustain *level* (held while the key is down), **R**elease time to silence after the key is let go. Three times and a level. | control | Adsr (L1.3), D6 |
| **LFO** | A low-frequency oscillator (≈0.01–20 Hz) used as a control: vibrato (on pitch), tremolo (on amp), wah (on cutoff). It's an oscillator you *don't* listen to directly. | control | Lfo (L1.11) |
| **Modulation** | One signal controlling a parameter of another module. Defined by a *source* (env, LFO, velocity, mod wheel, key), a *destination* (cutoff, pitch, op level), and an *amount*. FM is modulation where the source runs at audio rate and the destination is phase. | the connection itself | basic in L1.11, mod matrix in L4.1 |
| **Velocity / key tracking / mod wheel** | Per-note or performance inputs used as modulation sources: how hard you hit, which key you pressed, where the wheel sits. | control | L1.6, L2.x |
| **Voice** | One copy of the whole per-note signal path (operators → filter → amp, with their envelopes). Playing a 3-note chord needs 3 voices. **Mono** = one voice; **poly** = many; **voice count** = the most notes at once. | container | Voice / VoiceAllocator (L1.7), D8 |
| **Unison** | Several slightly detuned copies of a voice for *one* note, spread in stereo. Makes a thin sound thick. | container | L4.4 |

**The three families**, one line each:
- **Subtractive:** start bright (saw, square, noise), then carve with a filter whose cutoff moves.
  Intuitive because "less cutoff = darker" is monotonic.
- **FM/PM:** start pure (sines), and make brightness by wiggling one oscillator's phase with another.
  Brightness comes from the *modulator's level* (the index), and it isn't monotonic: partials grow,
  peak, shrink and come back as the index rises (Bessel functions, L1.4).
- **Additive:** build the spectrum directly, one sine per partial. Total control, too many knobs.

CFS is the first two glued together: an FM *source* into a subtractive *shaper* (D1, confirmed in L0.4).

## Three sounds from an init patch (Vital), reasoned, not played

Vital's signal flow, as I understand it from its documentation: up to 3 wavetable oscillators plus a
sampler → two filters (serial or parallel routing) → the effects rack → out. **ENV 1 is hard-wired to the
amplifier.** Everything else (ENV 2–6, LFOs, random, macros, velocity, key) is a modulation source you
drag onto a knob. The init patch is one oscillator, no filter, and an organ-like envelope (instant
attack, full sustain).

**1. Pluck** (short, bright at the start, darkening as it dies)
- Osc 1: saw. Filter 1 on: low-pass 12 dB, cutoff low (~300 Hz), resonance a little (~20%).
- ENV 1 (amp): A 0 ms, D ~400 ms, S 0, R ~200 ms. *No sustain is what makes it a pluck:* the note dies
  even when you hold the key.
- ENV 2 → filter cutoff, amount large (several octaves): A 0, D ~250 ms, S 0. The filter opens instantly
  and closes faster than the amp fades, so the tail is darker than the attack, like a real string.
- Velocity → ENV 2 amount: harder hits are brighter.
- Prediction: the *filter* decay, not the amp decay, is what you hear as "pluckiness". Shorten ENV 2's
  decay and it turns into a "blip"; lengthen it and it becomes a "harp".

**2. Pad** (slow, wide, evolving)
- Osc 1 and Osc 2: saws, Osc 2 detuned ~+7 cents; unison 4–6 voices with moderate detune and full stereo spread.
- Filter: low-pass, cutoff mid (~1–2 kHz), low resonance.
- ENV 1: A ~1.2 s, D ~1 s, S ~80%, R ~2 s. **This is the knob that makes it "slow"** (THINK #2).
- LFO 1 (slow, ~0.15 Hz) → filter cutoff, small amount: the slow drift is what reads as "evolving".
- Reverb + chorus in the FX rack.

**3. Bass** (solid, short-ish, focused)
- Osc 1: saw, plus Osc 2: square one octave down (or a sine sub), mono with legato/glide ~40 ms.
- Filter: low-pass 24 dB, cutoff ~400 Hz, resonance ~30%, *key tracking* on so high notes aren't dull.
- ENV 1: A 0, D ~300 ms, S ~70%, R ~60 ms (a short release keeps fast lines from smearing).
- ENV 2 → cutoff, moderate amount, D ~150 ms: a "pluck" on the front of each note.
- Prediction: turning resonance up while the filter envelope sweeps gives the "acid"/squelch character.

## [THINK] answers

1. **Cutoff down: what disappears first?** The top harmonics. A low-pass removes everything above the
   cutoff, and a saw's harmonics are spaced evenly in Hz (f, 2f, 3f, ...), so the *upper* harmonics go
   first and the fundamental goes last. Perceptually: first the "fizz", then the "edge", then the sound
   turns into something close to a sine.
2. **Why does a pad feel slow? Which knob?** The amp envelope's attack (and its release). A long attack
   means the sound arrives gradually; a long release means it lingers after you let go. A slow LFO on
   cutoff adds to it but isn't the main cause.
3. **Dexed: some operators change tone, others loudness. Why?** Carriers feed the output, so their level is
   *volume*. Modulators feed another operator's phase, so their level is the *modulation index*, i.e.
   *brightness/timbre*. Same knob, different job depending on where the operator sits in the algorithm.
   (This is also why every FM operator needs its own envelope: a modulator envelope is a filter envelope in disguise.)

## Experiment: 3 sounds from music, described in synth vocabulary
To revisit at L4.7.

1. **The DX7 "E.PIANO 1" factory patch** (all over 1980s ballads). FM: pairs of operators, each a
   modulator over a carrier at a 1:1-ish ratio. The modulators have fast-decaying envelopes, so the
   attack is bright and metallic (the "tine" ping) and quickly settles toward a mellow, near-sine body.
   One pair is slightly detuned for a chorus-like beat. No filter: the brightness envelope *is* the
   modulator envelopes.
2. **The brass riff in Van Halen's "Jump"** (an Oberheim OB-Xa, per many interviews). Subtractive:
   detuned saws (several voices per note), low-pass with a moderate filter envelope (the slight "bwah"
   swell at each chord), fast amp attack, high sustain. Polyphonic: the chords are the point.
3. **Acid bassline (TB-303, e.g. Phuture's "Acid Tracks").** Subtractive mono: one saw or square → a
   resonant low-pass with a short decay envelope on cutoff. High resonance, cutoff and envelope amount
   tweaked live. *Accent* boosts both volume and filter sweep on some steps; *slide* glides pitch between
   notes. The squelch is resonance plus a moving cutoff.

## How it works (the model I'm carrying forward)
Signal path vs control path is the main split. In code it maps to **audio-rate `tick()`** for the signal
path and **(eventually) lower-rate or smoothed parameters** for the control path. That's exactly the
`prepare / reset / tick` shape in ARCHITECTURE.md §3.

## What surprised me
- How much of "sound design" is really envelope design. All three recipes are ~70% envelope settings.
- That the FM "brightness knob" (modulator level) and the subtractive one (cutoff) behave so differently:
  one is monotonic, the other isn't. That's the whole argument for the hybrid (L0.4).
