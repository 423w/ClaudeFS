# L4.2: Richer operators: waveforms, ratios, FM aliasing (CFS)
*Date started / finished:* 2026-11-19 / 2026-11-19  ·  *Built by:* Claude

## Goal
Every operator gets a **waveform**: the sine, five TX81Z/OPL-style sine variants, and band-limited saw, square
and triangle (L1.8's PolyBLEP oscillators, now inside an operator). Then measure FM aliasing across the keyboard
and what 2× oversampling of the FM core would buy; build classic subtractive patches from saw carriers (the
**supersaw** L4.4 was waiting for) and a TX81Z-style one; snap ratios to harmonic values in the editor. Folded
in: three open items from L4.1/L4.4 (smoothed matrix slot amounts, the per-copy pitch cost of unison, the Synth
page's scrolling left column).

## Predictions (written before any L4.2 code)
1. **Waveforms as carriers** (one operator, no modulation; levels re the fundamental, or re the strongest
   partial when there's no fundamental). The sine variants are a sine with parts cut, flipped or doubled:
   - **Half sine** (the positive half, then silence): a fundamental at 0.5, then **even harmonics only**,
     2/(π(4k²−1)): 2nd −7.4 dB, 4th −21.4 dB. Soft, hollow-ish, a "pulse-like" sine.
   - **Abs sine** (|sin|, both halves positive): the period halves, so it **sounds an octave up**: partials at
     2, 4, 6... only, 4th −14 dB below the 2nd. Organ-ish.
   - **Quarter sine** (|sin| during the first and third quarters, 0 between): it **jumps** (from 1 to 0 at each
     quarter's end), so its harmonics fall only as 1/n, like a saw: the brightest sine variant, and it aliases.
   - **Alt sine** (a full sine at twice the speed in the first half, silence in the second): energy centred on the
     2nd harmonic with neighbours on both sides: nasal, vocal.
   - **Camel** (|alt sine|): partials around the 4th: thin and bright.
   - **Saw / square / triangle**: 1/n, odd 1/n, odd 1/n² (L1.8), band-limited (PolyBLEP) as carriers.
   All of them made **zero-mean** (their average subtracted), so a carrier never puts DC (thumps) in the output.
2. **As modulators at index 1** (a sine carrier at the same ratio): a sine gives Bessel sidebands J₀(1) = 0.77,
   J₁ = 0.44, J₂ = 0.11, J₃ = 0.02: four partials within −40 dB. A saw modulator has every harmonic, each making
   its own sidebands, so the spectrum gets much denser: I guess **~12 partials within −40 dB** and an inharmonic
   haze from where sidebands of different modulator harmonics overlap with different phases (they're all
   harmonic here, at ratio 1:1, so "inharmonic" only if the ratio isn't an integer).
3. **FM aliasing across the keyboard.** A bright 2-op patch (1:1, index 4, modulator feedback 1.5, which turns
   the modulator into a saw-like wave with endless harmonics), alias power re the whole signal: **C3 −90 dB, C5
   −55 dB, C7 −25 dB**. Running the FM core at 2× (operators at 96 kHz, then a 63-tap halfband down to 48 kHz):
   FM sidebands fall off a cliff beyond index + 1 (Bessel), so, as with tanh in L5.2, I expect a big gain where
   the cliff is above 24 kHz: **20–30 dB better at C6–C7**, little at low notes where there's nothing to fix.
4. **Saw carriers** through the filter: as clean as L1.8's PolyBLEP saw when unmodulated, so a **supersaw**
   (7 saws, unison 7, ~30 cents of spread) should sound like the classic: bright, wide, shimmering. Whether it
   "sounds right" is the owner's call; I'll check it renders with no clicks and plausible mono compatibility.
5. **Modulated saw carriers** (PM into a saw): PolyBLEP needs the phase step per sample, which phase modulation
   changes all the time. Using the *instantaneous* step (this sample's phase minus the last one's) keeps the
   correction right as long as the step stays between 0 and half a cycle; beyond that (deep modulation, the
   phase running backwards) the saw falls back to naive. I expect a lightly modulated saw (index ≤ 1) to keep
   most of PolyBLEP's alias reduction.
6. **Per-copy pitch cost** (L4.4: unison 8 cost 5.3× unison 1 on a patch with vibrato): computing the pitch ratio
   once per voice and multiplying each copy's cached detune ratio in should take out one `pow2` per copy per
   sample: **~25 % cheaper at unison 8**. (Not bit-identical for unison > 1: two products instead of one power.)
7. **Smoothed slot amounts**: a 1 kHz automation of a slot amount on a pitch route zippers today (steps every
   change); with a 20 ms glide the step is gone. Patches that don't move a slot amount: bit-identical.

## What actually happened
1. **Carriers: mostly right, two surprises** (`fss_render l4.2`; harmonics 1–8 re the strongest, then alias power
   of the same wave at 2.6 kHz):

   | wave | h1 | h2 | h3 | h4 | h5 | h6 | h7 | h8 | alias @2.6 kHz |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | Sine | 0 | – | – | – | – | – | – | – | −117 |
   | Half sine | 0 | −7.4 | – | −21.4 | – | −28.8 | – | −33.9 | −35 |
   | Abs sine | – | 0 | – | −14.0 | – | −21.3 | – | −26.4 | −27 |
   | Quarter sine | – | 0 | – | −8.7 | – | −12.6 | – | −15.3 | **−11** |
   | Alt sine | −1.4 | 0 | −5.9 | – | −18.3 | – | −24.9 | – | −33 |
   | Camel | 0 | – | −4.4 | −6.0 | −16.9 | – | −23.5 | −20.0 | −26 |
   | Saw / Square / Triangle | L1.8's 1/n, odd 1/n, odd 1/n² | | | | | | | | −28 / −32 / −53 |

   Half and abs sine are exactly the Fourier series I predicted (−7.4/−21.4, and −14.0 with no fundamental).
   **Quarter sine also sounds an octave up**: its two humps are identical, so its period is half a cycle (I only
   predicted "bright"). It's the worst aliaser of all (−11 dB at 2.6 kHz, worse than a naive saw), because it jumps.
   **Camel isn't "around the 4th"**: its strongest partial is the fundamental, because half of every cycle is
   silence, and that on/off gating is a strong fundamental by itself. All nine are zero-mean (tested to 2e-3).
2. **Modulators at index 1: the VA shapes are far denser than I guessed.** Partials within −40 dB: sine 4 (the
   Bessel prediction, right), half sine 6, abs 5, quarter 6, alt 8, camel 11, triangle 6, **square 49, saw 62**
   (predicted ~12). Every harmonic of a saw modulator makes its own sidebands, and at 1:1 they all land on the
   same harmonic grid, filling it up to Nyquist. A saw modulator is a "make everything bright" switch.
3. **FM aliasing across the keyboard, 1× vs 2× FM core** (A at index 4 with feedback → B, alias power re the
   whole signal; [ ] only below 16 kHz):

   | key | C2 | C3 | C4 | C5 | C6 | C7 | C8 |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | 1× | −111 | −112 | −104 | −67 [−98] | −39 [−61] | −20 [−34] | −10 [−13] |
   | 2× | −116 | −112 | −82 | −57 [−115] | −39 [−115] | −34 [−81] | −25 [−49] |

   Predicted C3 −90, C5 −55, C7 −25 at 1×: the shape is right (nothing below C4, then ~25–30 dB worse per
   octave), the low notes are cleaner than I guessed. **Below 16 kHz, 2× gains 18 dB at C5, 55 dB at C6, 46 at C7,
   36 at C8** (predicted 20–30 dB at C6–C7). Two things I didn't expect:
   - **Feedback is relative to the operator's output, level included.** My first two tries (feedback 1.5 and 0.7 on
     an operator at level 4) are effective feedback indices of 6 and 2.8: chaotic, a noise, not a periodic tone,
     so the meter read "−1 dB of aliasing" at every key. 0.25 × 4 = 1 is the saw-like regime.
   - **The 2× total is sometimes worse** (C4: −82 vs −104 dB), all of it above 16 kHz. Feedback FM is not sample-
     rate independent: its one-sample delay and two-sample average are shorter at 96 kHz, so the 2× oscillator is a
     little *brighter*, and its extra top harmonics land in the halfband's transition band (24–29 kHz), which
     folds to 19–24 kHz (the same floor as L5.2's Tube). Inaudible, and a reminder that turning 2× on changes a
     feedback patch's timbre slightly. Through the Engine (`test_opwaves.cpp`) a bright C7 is ≥ 20 dB cleaner.
4. **Supersaw**: renders without clicks; mono sum −1.6 dB against the stereo channels, side/mid −3.7 dB (a wide
   but mono-safe stack). Its first output level drove a 4-note chord into the soft clip (peak 1.0): lowered to
   0.18 (peak 0.85). Whether it sounds like *the* supersaw: the owner's ears (FL steps).
5. **PolyBLEP under PM: better than predicted.** Saw carrier, alias at 2.6 kHz, band-limited vs naive: index 0
   −28.2 / −11.9 dB, 0.5 −26.3 / −10.6, 1 −24.6 / −9.3, **3 −24.5 / −9.3**. The instantaneous step keeps ~15 dB of
   PolyBLEP's gain even at index 3: the phase rarely runs backwards there.
6. **Per-copy pitch: right direction, a third of the size.** Wide pad at unison 8, 16 voices: **1981 → 1788 µs**
   per block (**−9.7 %**, predicted −25 %). The `pow2` was gone, but the four `setFrequency` per copy per sample
   (the phasor increments) remain, and they were the bigger part.
7. **Slot smoothing: right, after a bug the test found.** The first version's glide got stuck 0.002 % short of
   its target forever (a float one-pole: (target − value)·k rounds to nothing), so it never "arrived" and the
   routing was recompiled every sample from then on. Now it snaps once within 0.01 % or when a step stops moving
   the value, and the test checks that the glide ends bit-identical to the target patch.
8. **CPU of the waveform switch: wrong at first.** The first version made every all-sine patch 2–4.5 % slower
   (E-piano chorus 198 → 208 µs per block): the waveform switch, inlined into `Operator::tick`, grew the FM
   voice's inner loop. Moving the non-sine waves into a `FSS_NOINLINE` function (new in `util/inline.h`) and
   dropping a per-sample store from the sine path: FM pad +0.7 %, Brass +0.4 %, E-piano chorus +1.5 % against
   b069de7, built the same way. Operator cost per sample: sine 4.2 ns, half sine 6.1, quarter 7.2, saw 7.5,
   triangle 9.0. The FM core at 2× costs exactly 2× (36.6 → 73.7 ns per voice copy).
9. **Tests 224/224** in Debug and Release (10 new). Block-size invariance with FM 2×, unison 3, a waveform change, a
   unison change and a gliding slot amount: bit-identical. Golden test (all-sine factory presets) bit-identical;
   `l1.7_piece.wav` `93d602f0…` unchanged; real-time audit 0 allocations; clap-validator 34/34.

**Exit criteria.**
- Measured aliasing reduction from oversampling ✅ (table 3: 18–55 dB below 16 kHz from C5 up).
- Classic VA-style and TX81Z-style patches saved ✅: **15 Saw lead, 16 Supersaw** (VA), **17 Wave bass, 18 Wave
  clav** (TX81Z-style). How good they sound is the owner's call.

## How it works
**Waveforms** (`dsp/op_waves.h`, an operator's `Wave`, id 1000 + 100·op + 13): Sine (the old operator, bit for
bit), five sine variants made from the sine table (half, abs, quarter, alt, camel; zero-mean, naive like the chips),
and L1.8's PolyBLEP saw, square and triangle, band-limited with the *instantaneous* phase step (this sample's total
phase minus the last). Non-sine waves run in an out-of-line function so the sine path stays as fast as before.

**FM 2×** (`FM 2x`, id 54, Voice group): every voice's FM core (all unison copies) runs at twice the sample rate and
a 63-tap halfband (the L5.2 one) brings each voice's stereo sum down; filter, amp and fade stay at the base rate.
Latency 16 samples (15.5 + one 2×-rate sample of padding), added to the drive's in `Engine::latencySamples()`, and
handled exactly like the drive's: committed at a restart in the plugin, at once in the standalone (where it
re-prepares the voices and stops what's sounding).

**Folded in**: matrix slot amounts glide over 20 ms (`Engine::glideSlotAmounts`); a unison voice computes its pitch
ratio once and each copy caches its detune ratio (`FmVoice::setPitchOffsetRatio`); a dragged operator Ratio knob
snaps to 1/8, 1/4, 1/2 and 1..32 (Ctrl = free; `snapRatio`, the knob keeps its own unsnapped drag position); the
Synth page fits without scrolling (a shorter algorithm drawing, flatter thumbnails, a wider left column).

## Alternatives rejected
- **Band-limiting the sine variants** (PolyBLEP/BLAMP at their jumps and corners): possible, but they're the
  "character" of a 4-op chip, mostly used as modulators where it doesn't matter, and FM 2× helps where it does.
- **Keeping their DC** (authentic to the chips): a carrier would thump at every note on and off.
- **A naive saw/square inside the operator** (cheaper, "FM-style"): 16 dB more aliasing at 2.6 kHz for 3 ns.
- **Oversampling only in the measurement tool**: the curriculum asks to add it to the voice, and it's worth
  55 dB at C6. As an option, default off: it doubles the FM cost and changes feedback timbres slightly.
- **Decimating once for all voices** (oversample the whole voice: filter, envelopes, everything at 2×): twice
  the cost of everything for no audible gain; the filter doesn't alias.
- **Coarse + fine ratio parameters** (DX7 style): two parameters per operator and a migration for every preset;
  snapping in the editor gives the same feel and keeps automation continuous.

## What surprised me
1. **Feedback is a level-dependent thing.** "Feedback 1.5" means nothing without the operator's level; two
   measurements were noise before I understood it.
2. **The saw modulator's density** (62 partials at index 1) and the camel's fundamental.
3. **2× can measure worse**, because feedback FM depends on the sample rate.
4. **A smoother that never arrives**: float one-poles stall a little short of their target. Every "snap when close"
   threshold has to be bigger than where it stalls.
5. **A 2–4 % slowdown from a branch that's never taken** on the common path (code size and inlining, not work).
