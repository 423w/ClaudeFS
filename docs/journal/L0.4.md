## Predictions (before building)
For y = sin(2π·fc·t + I·sin(2π·fm·t)), sidebands sit at fc ± k·fm with amplitude |J_k(I)|, and
negative frequencies fold back with a sign flip. So, by ratio fm/fc:

| ratio | predicted partials (× fc) | prediction in words |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1, 2, 3, 4, … | full harmonic series, saw-like as I grows |
| 2 | 1, 3, 5, 7, … | odd harmonics only: square/clarinet-ish, hollow |
| 3 | 1, 2, 4, 5, 7, 8, … | harmonic, every 3rd missing |
| 3.5 | 1, 2.5, 4.5, 6, 8, 9.5, … | all multiples of **0.5·fc**: pitch drops an octave, gappy series |
| 1.41 | 0.41, 1, 1.82, 2.41, 3.23, … | no common fundamental worth the name: **inharmonic, bell/metal** |

And for the index: number of strong sidebands ≈ I + 1 (Carson: bandwidth ≈ 2(I+1)·fm); the carrier
amplitude is J₀(I), which hits **zero at I ≈ 2.405**, so "more modulation" does *not* mean "more of
everything". The spectrum reshuffles.

## How it works

**FM vs subtractive, as a programmer would compare them:**

| | subtractive (saw → LPF) | FM / PM (2 sines) |
|---|---|---|
| brightness knob | cutoff | modulator level (index) |
| knob → spectrum | **monotonic**: lower cutoff = fewer highs, always | **non-monotonic**: partials rise, peak, fall, come back (J_k(I)) |
| harmonic or not | always harmonic (the saw is) | set by the *ratio*: integer = harmonic, otherwise inharmonic |
| DSP challenge | anti-aliased oscillators (L1.8), filters (L1.9–10) | almost none at first (pure sines); aliasing returns at high index × high pitch (L4.2) |
| cost of a voice | 1–3 oscillators + filter | 4 sines + 4 envelopes; cheap |
| sweet spot | bass, pads, leads, "analog" | bells, e-pianos, mallets, metallic and glassy tones, punchy FM bass |

**The subtractive bass, reasoned (step 2):** saw (or saw + square sub an octave down) → 24 dB/oct
low-pass, cutoff ~250 Hz with key tracking ~50 %, resonance ~25 %; filter envelope A 0 / D ~200 ms /
S 0, amount about +3 octaves; amp A 0 / D 300 ms / S 80 % / R 60 ms; mono, legato, glide ~30 ms. Every
knob does one predictable thing. That predictability is exactly what FM lacks, and why the hybrid puts a
filter *after* the operators: FM supplies spectra a saw can't, and the filter gives back a monotonic
"darker/brighter" control at the end of the chain.

## Alternatives rejected (for D1)
- **Pure FM (DX-style, no filter).** Authentic, and the operator envelopes already do "filter
  envelope" work, but FM-only sound design is famously hard to program. It also skips the filter lessons
  (L1.9–L1.10), the deepest DSP topic on the route.
- **Pure subtractive / VA.** The most intuitive to play, but its first months are spent fighting aliasing
  before anything sounds good, and it's the most crowded field ("sounds like everything else").
- **Wavetable-first.** Needs table content and mipmapping before it's interesting; L4.3 adds it later
  anyway.
- **Additive, physical modeling, granular.** Great detours (L4.6), not cores: too many parameters,
  too narrow a palette, or too sample-based.

## What surprised me
1. **The sideband at 0 Hz vanishes.** With ratio 1 the k = −1 sideband lands at exactly 0 Hz with
   |J₁(2)| = −4.8 dB predicted, and the measurement found nothing (−240 dB). In the sin-phase form a
   *sine* at 0 Hz is sin(0) = 0. With a cosine carrier it would be a DC offset instead. **Phase
   conventions are audible at the edges.** That's worth remembering in L1.4 (a DC offset in an
   FM voice is a real bug source: clicks on note-off, and headroom lost).
2. How exactly simple FM obeys theory: 0.01 dB everywhere. In L1.4 the C++ operator can be tested against
   **this same Bessel table**. The sketch already is the test oracle.
3. Ratio 3.5 drops the perceived pitch an octave (implied fundamental 200 Hz, not 400). In a real patch,
   that's a "why is my patch playing in the wrong octave?" moment waiting to happen.

## What CFS v1 will be (exit criterion)
**CFS v1 is a polyphonic FM-core hybrid synthesizer, played as a CLAP plugin in FL Studio.** Each voice
runs four sine operators (phase modulation, each with its own ratio, level and envelope, feedback on at
least one) whose routing is a small modulation matrix filled in from algorithm presets. The operators
feed a per-voice virtual-analog state-variable filter with its own envelope and key tracking, then an amp
stage shaped by an envelope and velocity. LFOs and per-note sources (velocity, key, mod wheel, aftertouch)
modulate it. Voices are summed into a short global FX chain (chorus → delay → reverb at minimum). All DSP
lives in a standard-library-only `fss_core`, rendered offline first, then played live, then hosted.
Operator count, voice count and the envelope model are still open (D6–D8). The sentences above are
expectations, not decisions.
