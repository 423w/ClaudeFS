# L1.5: Operators, algorithms & feedback: the FM voice (CFS)
*Date started / finished:* 2026-11-17 / 2026-11-17  ·  *Built by:* Claude

## Goal
Four operators in configurable routings: `Algorithm` (data) + `FmVoice` (execution), feedback, eight
preset algorithms, and e-piano / bass / organ renders. Decide D7; confirm D1.

## Predictions (before building)
- The generic matrix loop must reproduce a hand-wired chain **bit-exactly** (same operations, same order).
- One operator with self-feedback β (y = sin(ωt + β·y)) has harmonics aₙ = 2·Jₙ(nβ)/(nβ) (Kepler's-equation
  series). Relative to h1, for β = 0.5: h2 −12.5, h3 −21.5, h4 −29.1, h5 −35.9, h6 −42.1 dB; β = 1.0: −7.9,
  −12.6, −15.9, −18.5, −20.6 (a saw is −6.0, −9.5, −12.0). Valid only for β ≤ 1; above that the ideal
  waveform gets a vertical jump and the formula breaks down.
- Feedback sweep 0 → 4: sine → saw-like → noise; brightness rises by orders of magnitude.

## How it works
```
for dst in A..D:   pm = Σ mod[src][dst] · (src < dst ? out[src] : ½(prev[src] + prev2[src]))
                   out[dst] = op[dst].tick(pm)
y = Σ carrier[i] · out[i];   prev2 = prev; prev = out
```
Presets are numbered so modulators come before what they modulate; anything else is automatically a
one-sample-delayed back edge. The two-sample average is (1 + z⁻¹)/2, a filter with a zero exactly at
Nyquist: it kills the fs/2 "flip-flop" that raw one-sample feedback falls into at high amounts.

Files: `core/include/fss/synth/algorithm.h` (Algorithm, `kAlgorithms`, diagrams), `fm_voice.h`,
`tests/test_fm_voice.cpp`, `tools/render/l1_5.cpp`.

## Alternatives rejected
- **A topological sort of the matrix** each time it changes. Correct but unnecessary: "A→D order, back edges
  delayed" handles every matrix, including cycles, with zero bookkeeping. The cost is that the numbering
  matters (B→A would be delayed by one sample, A→B wouldn't); presets just number correctly.
- **One `tick` function per algorithm** (code, not data). Fastest and easiest to read per algorithm, but
  8 functions and no free matrix. Revisit only if profiling says so.
- **Feedback as a separate per-voice parameter** (DX7 has one feedback knob, on one operator). The matrix
  diagonal generalizes it at no cost; a UI can still expose just one.
- **Automatic carrier normalization in the voice.** Explicit gains in the data are visible and editable;
  presets simply store 1/N.

## What surprised me
1. The feedback formula matched to 0.1 dB at β = 0.5, through a one-sample delay *and* the DX averaging.
   At 110 Hz one sample is 0.8° of phase, which is effectively instantaneous.
2. Above β ≈ 1 the measured spectrum is brighter than any smooth formula predicts, and by β ≈ 3 it's noise
   (centroid 10+ kHz). "Feedback = saw" holds only in a narrow band around β ≈ 1–1.5.
3. The `std::initializer_list` + `constexpr` table builder worked first time under MSVC. Edge lists read far
   better than 4×4 literal matrices.

## [THINK] answers
- **Fixed algorithms vs free matrix (player / programmer)?** A player gets a small, learnable menu with fixed
  algorithms, and endless confusion with a free 16-knob matrix (L4.1 needs good UI). A programmer gets one
  data structure for both: presets *are* matrices.
- **CPU when every op modulates every op?** 16 multiply-adds per sample per voice vs ~1–3 for presets: noise
  next to 4 sine lookups and 4 envelopes. At 4 ops it doesn't matter; at 8 ops (64 entries) it might.
- **Is feedback order-dependent? One sample at 20 kHz?** Yes: which ops see "this sample" vs "last sample"
  depends on numbering. One sample at 48 kHz is 20.8 µs. For a 110 Hz tone that's 0.8° of phase (irrelevant);
  for a 20 kHz tone it's 150°, so feedback at high pitch behaves very differently from the ideal. That's
  another reason FM gets harsh up high (L4.2).

## Decisions made
- **D7** (decided): 4 ops, free matrix, 8 preset algorithms, A→D evaluation with averaged back edges.
- **D1** (confirmed): FM-core hybrid. The owner's listening is the real test (see DECISIONS.md).
