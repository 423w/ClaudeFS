# L5.5: Reverb: Schroeder → Freeverb → FDN (CFS)
*Date started / finished:* 2026-11-19 / 2026-11-19  ·  *Built by:* Claude

## Goal
A reverb in the FX chain, after the delay and before the width (D16's order), whose **Decay knob is the
measured RT60**. On the way: a Schroeder reverb and Freeverb as reference implementations (renders only), so
their flaws can be heard and measured, then **D17**: the "real" one.

## Predictions (written before any L5.5 code)
1. **Schroeder** (4 parallel combs of 29.7–43.7 ms, 2 series allpasses of 5 and 1.7 ms, the 1962 values):
   metallic and fluttery. Its comb gains are set from the RT60 directly (g = 10^(−3τ/RT60)), so its decay should
   be accurate (within 5 %), but its **echo density** stays low: 4 combs make only ~100 echoes per second at
   first. On the normalized echo density (NED: the fraction of samples beyond one standard deviation in a 20 ms
   window, divided by 0.3173, the Gaussian value; 1 = "sounds like noise") I expect Schroeder to stay below
   **0.5** for the first 100 ms.
2. **Freeverb** (8 damped combs + 4 allpasses per channel): denser (NED ~0.8 by 100 ms), smoother, still a
   little metallic on a bell. Its "room size" is a feedback, not a time: at 0.84 (its default) and ~30 ms average
   comb length, RT60 ≈ −3·0.030/log10(0.84) ≈ **1.2 s** (at 44.1 kHz tunings; at 48 kHz the combs are shorter).
3. **The FDN** (D17, see below): 8 delay lines, 50–100 ms, mixed by a Householder matrix; in front, a 4-stage
   diffuser (delays + Hadamard mixing). Each line's loop gain g = 10^(−3·d/RT60) makes the decay uniform, so
   **broadband RT60 within ±5 % of the knob** with damping off, from 0.3 s to 10 s. With damping, the RT60 at
   1 kHz stays within ±10 %, and at 8 kHz it's shorter (damping 50 % aims at about 0.55× at Nyquist; I guess
   **0.7×** at 8 kHz). NED reaches **0.9 within 50 ms**, thanks to the diffuser (8 × 4 stages = 4096 paths).
4. **Modulation** (each line's length wobbling by a fraction of a millisecond, slow and at different rates)
   breaks up the fixed modes that make long tails ring. I expect a long tail's spectrum (1 s into the decay) to
   get measurably **flatter**: its spectral peak-to-mean drops by a few dB with modulation on.
5. **Stereo**: the left and right outputs take different lines with different signs, so the tail's L/R
   correlation should be near **0** (|ρ| < 0.1): a wide reverb from a mono bell.
6. **Mix law: equal power** (a reverb is uncorrelated with the dry sound, like the delay and the chorus).
7. **CPU** (Release, stereo, 48 kHz): ~**200 ns** per sample (8 cubic reads, 32 diffuser taps, 4 Hadamards,
   a Householder, 8 one-poles). About 1 % of a core.
8. **D17 prediction**: I'll pick the FDN over Dattorro's plate, because its decay is *specified* (per-line
   gains from RT60, the exit criterion) instead of tuned, and it scales (lines, size) without retuning.

## What actually happened
1. **Three reverbs at a 2 s setting** (`fss_render l5.5`; RT60 by Schroeder backward integration, T30; NED in
   20 ms windows; the Python tool `tools/analyze/rt60.py` gives the same RT60s to 0.01 s):

   | | RT60 broadband | 250 Hz / 1 kHz / 4 kHz / 12 kHz | NED at 25 / 50 / 100 / 200 ms |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | Schroeder | 2.00 s | 1.98 / 1.99 / 2.00 / 2.00 | 0.13 / 0.28 / 0.20 / 0.22 |
   | Freeverb (room 0.5) | 1.30 s | 1.29 / 1.31 / 1.30 / 1.30 | 0.04 / 0.44 / 0.76 / 0.78 |
   | FDN (CFS) | 1.89 s | 1.99 / 2.00 / 1.97 / 1.68 | 0.04 / **0.99** / 1.01 / 1.06 |

   - **Schroeder: right.** Exact decay (its combs are set from the RT60), and sparse: NED never passes 0.3. On
     the bell (`l5.5_bell_schroeder.wav`) it's a fluttering, metallic rattle.
   - **Freeverb: right.** RT60 1.30 s against my 1.24 s estimate (its combs are 31.2 ms at 48 kHz); NED 0.78 at
     100 ms, denser but not Gaussian, and it never quite gets there.
   - **FDN: right on density** (0.99 at 50 ms, prediction 0.9), **half right on decay**: see 2.
2. **The knob vs the measured RT60** (damping 0, mod 30 %):

   | knob | 0.3 s | 1 s | 2 s | 5 s | 10 s |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | 1 kHz band | 0.30 (−0.1 %) | 0.99 (−0.9 %) | 2.00 (−0.1 %) | 4.95 (−0.9 %) | 9.88 (−1.2 %) |
   | broadband | 0.29 (−4.4 %) | 0.94 (−5.9 %) | 1.89 (−5.5 %) | 4.72 (−5.7 %) | 9.42 (−5.8 %) |

   **Exit criterion (within ~10 %) ✅** everywhere, but broadband misses my ±5 % prediction by a hair, and always
   on the short side. The bands say why: 250 Hz to 4 kHz are within 1–3 %; only the top octave decays faster
   (12 kHz: 1.68 s instead of 2). Each pass through a line goes through its cubic read, which loses a fraction of a
   dB near Nyquist; at ~15 passes per second that adds up. With the modulation on the fraction sweeps, so the loss
   is a little larger (12 kHz, 5 s setting: 4.33 s without modulation, 3.94 s with). A white impulse puts half its
   energy above 12 kHz, which is why the *broadband* figure feels it. RT60 is quoted at mid frequencies in
   acoustics, so that's where the knob is exact; the test checks the 1 kHz band to 5 % and broadband to 10 %.
3. **Damping: right in shape.** 2 s decay, RT60 at 250 Hz / 1 kHz / 4 kHz / 8 kHz: damping 0 → 1.99 / 2.00 /
   1.97 / 1.85; 40 % → 1.98 / 1.99 / 1.90 / 1.70; 100 % → 1.96 / 1.87 / 1.35 / 0.98 s. At 8 kHz, 50 % gives
   about 0.8× (predicted 0.7×); the lows stay at the knob.
4. **Modulation: right in direction, smaller than predicted.** Late-tail spectral roughness (Welch spectrum of
   1.0–2.5 s of an 8 s tail, minus its median-smoothed self, std dev 200 Hz–5 kHz): **2.28 dB → 1.89 dB** with
   mod 30 %. Pure noise would read ~1.05 dB with this many averages, so the modes' share drops from ~2.0 to
   ~1.6 dB: real, but not the "few dB" I guessed. An 8-line FDN with 0.5 s of total delay is already fairly dense
   in modes; the modulation matters most on long tails and pure tones (try a held sine through a 10 s setting).
5. **Stereo: right.** Tail L/R correlation **0.031** (predicted |ρ| < 0.1).
6. **Level calibration**: the first output gain (0.35) left the wet signal **12.9 dB below** the dry one on noise
   at a 1.5 s decay; now 1.5, which measures −0.2 dB. Equal-power mix as predicted.
7. **CPU: wrong, then fixed.** First version **557 ns** per stereo sample (2.7 % of a core), against my 200. Half
   of it was the diffuser: 32 general `DelayLine`s, each push/read with its clamps and fractional bookkeeping, for
   delays that never change. One ring per stage with the 8 channels interleaved: **260–275 ns** (1.3 %), output
   bit-identical. The Engine with the reverb off: no change against 1bba8f3 built the same way (FM pad 367 vs
   367–374 µs per 16-voice block, E-piano chorus 192–195 vs 191–193).
8. **The L5.3 open item, folded in: the delay's 192 kHz reset.** `DelayLine::reset()` used to fill the buffer:
   210 µs on the audio thread at 192 kHz whenever a slot put its delay to sleep. It now just forgets
   (`written_ = 0`), and reads that reach past what was written since read zeros. Measured: **0.0 µs** for the delay
   and **4.6 µs** for the reverb (its small diffuser rings are still cleared outright). Bit-identical: a test
   compares a used-then-reset line with a fresh one for all three interpolations, and the golden test (E-piano
   chorus goes through the chorus's delay lines) still passes.
9. **Tests 214/214** in Debug and Release (5 new). Block-size invariance through the Engine with the reverb on and
   its size, decay, pre-delay, damping and switch changing: bit-identical. Real-time audit 0 allocations;
   clap-validator 34/34; `l1.7_piece.wav` `93d602f0…` unchanged. New factory preset **14 "Bell hall"**.

**Exit criteria.**
- Measured RT60 within ~10 % of the setting ✅ (1 kHz band within 1.2 %, broadband within 5.9 %, 0.3 to 10 s).
- "A reverb you'd use on your FM bells instead of your DAW's stock reverb": **the owner's call**, by ear
  (`renders/l5.5/l5.5_bell_{dry,schroeder,freeverb,fdn}.wav`, the "Bell hall" preset, FL steps below).

## How it works
`fx/reverb.h`: pre-delay (whole samples) → one-pole low cut → **diffuser**: 8 channels (L into the even ones, R into
the odd ones, ×½), 4 stages, each a fixed delay per channel (spread over 4/8/16/32 ms), a shuffle (i·3 + s + 1 mod 8),
fixed signs and an 8-point Hadamard (/√8) → **loop**: 8 cubic-read delay lines of 40–100 ms × size, each through
its Jot absorption filter (one-pole low-pass: DC gain g = 10^(−3d/(RT60·fs)), Nyquist gain for RT60 × (1 − 0.8 ×
damping)), mixed by a Householder reflection (x − (2/N)Σx, one sum), plus the diffused input → **outputs**: two
orthogonal ±1 patterns over the 8 lines (late) and over the diffuser (early, ×0.5) → equal-power mix. Each line's
length wobbles ±0.5 ms × Mod with its own LFO (0.1–1 Hz, a parabolic sine: no `sin` per line per sample).

Chain: drive → crush → chorus → flanger → phaser → delay → **reverb** → width → output (D16's planned slot).
It spills its tail when switched off. Params 270–277: on, decay (s), size (%), pre-delay (ms), damping (%),
mod (%), low cut (Hz), mix (%). The FX page now wraps its eight panels into two rows of four.

Reference designs (renders only, `tools/render/l5_5.cpp`): Schroeder 1962 (4 combs + 2 allpasses) and Freeverb
(8 damped combs + 4 "allpasses", Jezar's tunings and gains). Measurement: `tools/render/rt60.h` (C++, used by the
tests) and `tools/analyze/rt60.py` (Python, plots EDCs and echo density, `--roughness`).

## Alternatives rejected
- **Dattorro's plate** [DATTORRO]: gorgeous and cheap, but a fixed topology tuned at 29.8 kHz (every delay length
  has to be rescaled per sample rate), and its "decay" is a feedback gain, not a time: meeting "RT60 within 10 %
  of the knob" would mean calibrating a curve. The FDN states its decay directly. (D17 entry.)
- **Freeverb as the shipped reverb**: simple and recognizable, but sparse and metallic on bells (NED 0.78), with a
  room size that's a feedback amount.
- **More lines (16)**: denser modes, twice the cost; 8 lines + a 4-stage diffuser already reach NED 1 by 50 ms.
- **Linear interpolation in the loop**: cheaper, but its high-frequency loss compounds per pass (L5.4 measured
  −3 dB at fs/4 for a half-sample fraction): the top octave would die far faster. Cubic loses much less, still
  measurably (point 2 above). Allpass interpolation is flat but stateful, bad for moving delays.
- **Normalizing the wet level by decay** (equal energy at any RT60): then a long reverb would be quieter while it
  builds; I kept the natural behaviour (longer decay = more reverb), calibrated at 1.5 s.

## What surprised me
1. **The broadband RT60 is 6 % short, for a reason I didn't model**: the interpolation inside the loop is a filter
   too, and filters in feedback loops compound. Measuring per band found it in one run.
2. **The diffuser, not the loop, was the CPU hog.** 32 tiny delay lines with general-purpose reads cost more than 8
   modulated cubic reads.
3. **Heap corruption again, in the scenario code**: an unclamped last block (the exact gotcha in the owner's notes).
   A crash before any output, because a piped stdout is fully buffered and dies with the process.
4. **Schroeder's reverb is accurate and still bad**: the decay is perfect, the density isn't. RT60 alone says nothing
   about whether a reverb sounds good.

## Compare with your version
- **Which algorithm**, and does your decay knob mean seconds? Measure your RT60 per band: is your top octave short too?
- **Echo density**: what NED does yours reach at 50 ms? (`python tools/analyze/rt60.py your_ir.wav --out x.png`)
- **Damping**: a fixed low-pass per line (Freeverb's way) or Jot's per-line filters? Try long decays: with a fixed
  low-pass the mids' RT60 drops too.
- **Modulation**: how much, and can you measure what it does? My roughness number moved less than I expected.
- **CPU**: where does yours spend it?

## Decisions made
- **D17 → FDN**: 8 lines, Householder feedback, a 4-stage Hadamard diffuser, Jot damping, modulated lines.
  Full entry in DECISIONS.md.

## Open questions / for later
- A "freeze" (decay → ∞, input muted) would be a few lines: gain 1, no input. Nice for pads.
- Early reflections are just the diffuser's output; a tapped early-reflection pattern per room size could make
  small rooms more convincing.
